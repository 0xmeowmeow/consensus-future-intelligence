Each held pointer pulls towards its position with a capped force. The LLM supplies weights for possible next characters; those letters pull on the glass. The tiny model adds a weaker field of its own. The server combines these forces 20 times per second.

A character is written only after the glass settles over it for 0.65 seconds, with a human holding and a fresh LLM contribution present. Nobody chooses a complete answer in advance. The field colours show the two models’ preferences; the lines show people’s hands.

The origin model has random weights. It cannot yet speak coherently. The pretrained guest is a separate participant. This pilot has one guest, with bounded influence.

The room is listening.

Two independent “keep” votes and no objections make this a training example. Every view stays in the record.

Where does this work go?

The verified control becomes a training example immediately. The second answer needs at least two independent identities agreeing, with at least 75% agreement. Later disagreement can remove it from future training batches. Existing model weights are not automatically unlearned.

This local pilot distinguishes browser identities, not unique people. These controls check a small task; they are not a proven defence against coordinated manipulation.
