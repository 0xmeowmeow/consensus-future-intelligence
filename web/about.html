A small mechanism with an open outcome

What is actually
happening here?

The question is whether a shared ritual can produce both an encounter and useful evidence for training a model. This first build makes the causal chain inspectable: work → tokens → participation → motion → recorded encounter → review → training → new model.

Three participants, three roles.

Humans contribute physical pulls through a mouse, touch screen or arrow keys. A local pretrained language model earns an entry token and suggests weighted next characters. A separate 99,504-parameter character GRU starts with random weights and supplies its own smaller field. Its checkpoint changes only when someone trains and adopts a candidate.

There is no stored answer waiting to be revealed. The glass integrates all current forces on the server. When it rests near a character for 0.65 seconds, with a held human hand and a fresh LLM contribution, that character is written. The LLM then responds to the prefix that the circle actually produced.

The modem sound is a synthesised ritual. No telephone call is made.

What the entity learns.

A work packet pairs a fresh, generated movement control with an independently interpreted question. A correct control earns one token and becomes one training example. A question with at least two agreeing identities and at least 75% agreement becomes a provisional example. Objections remain in the vote history. Encounter text requires two “keep” reflections and no objections.

Training runs 160 AdamW steps on a frozen set of eligible examples, starting from the active checkpoint. Before and after, it is tested on the same generated movement questions, excluding exact overlap with its training input. Those narrow scores may improve, fall or remain unchanged. Lower training loss does not prove intelligence or alignment.

Checkpoint downloads contain the actual weights, their hash, architecture, parent and measured results, plus code to run next-character inference. Candidate adoption is manual in this pilot; model PR voting and distributed training are future work.

What the prototype does not establish.

Different browser identities are not proof of different people. Consensus is not proof of truth. A successful control is not proof that every other answer is honest. This is a local experiment for a small trusted circle, not a public, sabotage-resistant economy.

Removing a disputed example changes future training eligibility. It does not remove its influence from existing checkpoints. All checkpoint ancestry and frozen batches are retained locally so that a later rebuild can be explicit.

One guest and one entity currently share the board with human browsers. Payments, volunteer devices, model forks with community governance, arbitrary agent connections and additional digital ghosts are not connected yet.

Data is saved in the local server’s state directory: pseudonymous names, work, ledger entries, questions, chats, pushes, reflections and model history. The circle shares its visible messages and encounter records with connected participants. Clearing a browser cookie loses access to that identity.
